Duck Eggs vs. Chicken Eggs: What's the Difference?

If you've only ever cooked with chicken eggs, duck eggs can be a fun surprise. Here's what's different and how to use them.

The quick answer

Duck eggs are bigger than chicken eggs, have thicker shells, and have a larger yolk compared to the white. They taste a lot like chicken eggs, just richer. Bakers often love them, and anyone who likes a runny yolk usually does too.

Side by side

Chicken eggsDuck eggs
SizeStandardNoticeably larger
ShellThinner, cracks easilyThicker and tougher
YolkSmaller share of the eggBigger share of the egg
TasteMild, familiarRicher, creamier
Best forEveryday cookingBaking, custards, fried or soft eggs

Why bakers like duck eggs

Because duck eggs have more yolk, they add richness to cakes, custards and pasta dough. Many bakers say their cakes come out taller and moister. Since duck eggs are bigger, a common swap is two duck eggs for every three chicken eggs. For exact results, weigh them: recipes that list eggs by weight make the swap easy.

Tips for cooking with duck eggs

  • Crack them firmly. The shell and the thin skin under it are tougher. Tap on a flat counter rather than the edge of a bowl.
  • Go a little lower on heat. Duck egg whites can turn rubbery if you cook them fast and hot. Medium-low heat works well for frying and scrambling.
  • Give boiled eggs a bit more time. They're larger, so add a minute or two compared to a chicken egg.

A note on allergies

Some people who are allergic to chicken eggs also react to duck eggs. If you have an egg allergy, check with your doctor before trying them.
